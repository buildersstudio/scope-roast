# Lifeline

## Background
Eleven years as a product designer, the last four at a UK health insurer working on member-facing tools. Not a clinician, and not a care professional.

## Connection to the problem
My mother was diagnosed with vascular dementia in January 2023. For nineteen months I coordinated her care from 200 miles away: four carers on rotation, two GPs, a district nurse, a social worker, and my brother. I kept it all in a WhatsApp group and a shared note that eventually ran to 8,000 words. In August 2023 she was double-dosed on her blood pressure medication because two carers each thought the other had skipped it.

## Ideal customer
Adult children aged 45 to 60 in the UK who are the primary coordinator for a parent living at home with dementia, where paid carers are involved but there is no case manager. Roughly the moment after diagnosis and before residential care.

## Quantifying the pain
I logged my own time for three months: 6.5 hours a week on coordination, not care. Of the 40 families I ran the manual version for, the median was 5 hours a week, and 23 of them reported at least one medication or appointment error in the previous quarter.

## Existing attempts
Jointly is the closest, a shared calendar and notes app for carers. CareZone was the US equivalent and shut down. Most families use WhatsApp plus a paper diary in the kitchen. The councils that offer anything offer a PDF.

## Why those fall short
Jointly and its peers are shared containers. They assume somebody keeps them updated, and the person who would keep them updated is the exhausted coordinator. Nothing reads the mess that already exists, the WhatsApp thread and the photographed pill chart, and turns it into a state everyone can see. That is the whole job.

## Solution
One thing: the carer sends a WhatsApp voice note or photo after each visit, and the app maintains a single live picture of medication given, appointments, and changes, visible to everyone. No new app for the carers to learn.

## Validation
340 paying subscribers at 9 pounds a month, 71 percent retained past month six. Before that I ran it by hand over WhatsApp for 40 families for five months. 12 percent of subscribers came from a single dementia carers Facebook group where I answered questions for a year before I had anything to sell.

## Distribution
Three UK dementia carer Facebook groups totalling 90,000 members where I am already a known participant. Two Admiral Nurse services have asked to trial it with their families.

## Next steps
Get one Admiral Nurse service to a formal referral agreement by November. Hold retention above 65 percent at month six as I scale past 1,000 subscribers. If retention drops below 55 percent the product is not load bearing and I stop.
